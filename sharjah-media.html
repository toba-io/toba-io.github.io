What we found

Count the energy
the battery uses.

With cooling, pumps, and controls included, the insulated LFP system delivered 77–81% of the energy taken in, compared with 61–64% for VRFB. The container envelope made a large difference: white coating and 50 mm insulation reduced annual LFP cooling electricity by roughly five to six times compared with bare dark steel.

In the base scenario—an assumed import price of $0.12/kWh and no payment for exports—neither battery broke even over 20 years. PV alone was financially positive under the study assumptions. Our conclusion was to evaluate PV and storage separately, and size storage around clearly defined services.

We also asked what it would take for storage to pay. Smaller storage, a better enclosure and extra revenue all reduce the loss. With a hypothetical evening export price of $0.60/kWh, retail LFP becomes profitable with ideal forecasts, while VRFB would need about $0.97/kWh.
